It isn't just iPhones that could be announced today. The iPhone event usually comes with a cohort of other tech launches like AirPods or the Apple Watch. You can see a full list below:

The camera upgrades in the iPhone 18 Pro
The announcements today have interesting elements for creatives, specifically those who shoot, edit or design regularly. But you need to look to the software rather than the hardware.
The iPhone 18 Pro's camera app is a crucial update.
Code found in the iOS 27 beta shows five new pro tools: exposure scopes (real-time waveform and histogram overlays), focus peaking to highlight what's actually sharp before you shoot, proper manual focus control, repeating self-timer shots, and aperture-based lens correction tied to the rumoured variable aperture on the main sensor.
That means a physically adjustable aperture gives filmmakers actual depth-of-field control rather than the software-simulated bokeh Portrait mode has relied on for years.
Manual focus, notably, already exists in Apple's separate Final Cut Camera app; if it's genuinely moving into the standard Camera app, then Apple has realised that creators want pro tools without a second download.

How will Apple handle Siri?
(Image credit: Apple)
The iPhone Duo aside, the other most crucial announcement today will be for whatever is going on with Siri.
With Apple deemed behind on AI, 
Ternus has to be careful on his approach to AI now (read my opinion on this here
). So I'm invested in what new and improved capabilities Siri will have that stay true to the ethical concerns of Apple's strategy so far.
The new Apple Intelligence-powered Siri AI was already unveiled at WWDC back in June. This turned out to be a dedicated Siri app, multi-turn conversational ability, on-screen awareness, personal context, and app actions, built on a partnership with 
Google's Gemini running some of the backend
. But announcing it and releasing it haven't been the same thing. The rebuilt Siri was originally promised for iOS 26.4 back in March, then moved to May, then pushed back again to pair with iOS 27. This is exactly the software this event, and today's hardware, is built around.
A couple of points we know about already. Siri AI won't be available in the EU or China at launch, and Apple has confirmed usage will come with daily limits, with iCloud+ subscribers getting a higher cap, which means that the new Siri's powers won't be "free and unlimited". And given how many times this has been delayed already, we aren't totally sure it'll even happen today.

Is Apple's HomePad about to finally drop?
The HomePad was first rumoured for early 2025, then early 2026, then pushed back again as Apple waited for the Apple Intelligence-powered Siri to be ready. Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported the hardware itself has sat finished for months, with the delay coming entirely from software, and named September as the earliest realistic launch time now that new Siri is ready.
If it does land today, expect a 7-inch square display with a front-facing camera, running on an A18 chip, which is the same silicon in the iPhone 16. Reports point to two versions: one designed to mount on a wall, another with a speaker base for a desktop or countertop. Pricing may be around $350, though that looks optimistic next to the standard HomePod's own $349 tag.
Nobody's even settled on what to call it yet. Will it be HomePad, HomeHub, or the somewhat uninspired "HomePod Display"? These have all been floated, and reporting on the internal OS (reportedly codenamed "Charismatic") suggests Apple is still working out whether this is a family-shared status board or something closer to a proper smart-home hub to rival Amazon and Google's displays. Given how many times this device has slipped, we'll believe it when Ternus holds it up on stage.

The foldable iPhone Ultra's competition
(Image credit: Huawei)
So what's Apple up against exactly?
The foldable phone market in 2026 is starting to look pretty mature. Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra is the most polished all rounder, while Google’s Pixel 10 Pro Fold has the edge for software and AI, and challengers like Honor’s Magic V6 and 
Huawei’s Mate XT Ultimate
 are pushing design boundaries – the later being a tri‑fold spectacle (pictured).
The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra remains the benchmark with its wide book‑style design, refined hinge, deep ecosystem support and multi‑window multitasking. Google’s Pixel 10 Pro Fold undercuts Samsung slightly on price while offering the most advanced AI features and computational photography, though its hardware feels less premium.
The 
Honor Magic V6
 is the thinnest foldable yet, with IP68/IP69 durability, but its $1,749 / £1,999 starting price and smaller ecosystem limit appeal. Huawei’s Mate XT Ultra opens to a massive 10.2‑inch screen, which looks impressive but isn't really the most practical design.
For clamshell fans, there's the Galaxy Z Flip8 and Motorola Razr Ultra. Samsung offers the most refined hinge, while Motorola packs standout battery life and charging. Xiaomi’s Mix Fold 4 and OnePlus’s Open 2 compete on price, with Xiaomi leaning on Leica‑tuned cameras.

Crease? What crease?
The crease on the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 3 
(Image credit: Future)
Apple will be hoping that its characteristic decision to sit out the initial rise of foldable phones pays off. The Cupertino tech giant, it likes to arrive late to the party but make a big entrance. In this case, the party trick is expected to be the unveiling of a solution to what's often touted as foldable phones' big visual problem: the crease.
Reports have suggested that the foldable iPhone Ultra will have an almost invisible crease thanks to the innovation of a durable liquid metal hinge designed to spread bending tension, combined with a dual-layer ultra-thin glass panel that starts thinner at the fold and becomes thicker at the outer edges for impact resistance. The crease is said to be just 0.15mm deep, setting a new benchmark for folding screens.

Will the iPhone Ultra be a health hazard?
(Image credit: 9to5Mac)
A physical therapist fears that Apple's 
folding iPhone design could be a health risk
. You might have heard of 'iPhone pinky' – finger soreness due to resting the phone on it. Martin Ratcliffe suggests that the extra weight of the rumoured new device that we're calling the iPhone Ultra could be bad news for our thumbs.
He told mobile phone recycling comparison site 
Compare and Recycle
: “After several hours of use, the continued strain from the added weight can lead to fatigue and increase tension on the tissues, particularly the tissue around the thumb, which is the main finger used to navigate devices. Furthermore, the grip position may change because of the phone's foldable design. Overall, although the additional weight may be small, the repetitive strain and low-grade tension may lead to further stress and injury to surrounding tissues and joints.”
A little alarmist? There is some precedent for foldable phone users complaining of hand strain. On a Reddit page for 
Samsung Galaxy
 Fold owners, 
users have asked
 whether others have experiencing aching or stinging hands.

So, why no iPhone 18?
It's unprecedented for Apple 
not
 to launch an updated version of the base iPhone in September – and something that hasn't happened since the dawn of the iPhone itself.
(Image credit: Getty Images)
So, why has it made this decision? A few factors have been cited. For a start, the iPhone line up has become pretty unwieldily. With the advent of the iPhone Air and iPhone 17e, plus the upcoming iPhone Ultra, that's a hell of a lot of iPhone models – and arguably too many to update in a single sitting. It makes sense, if only for simplicity, for Apple to spread the launches over the year.
But it's also possible that Apple is trying to push users towards its higher end models. With those RAM price increases, users are getting their bank balances squeezed more than ever. In that context, users might be much more likely to opt for the cheapest model.

Expect a significant price rise
New reports suggest the iPhone 18 Pro could land with a significantly higher price tag, and it's all down to the components inside. Apple is expected to move to TSMC's 2-nanometre process for its A20 Pro chip, a shift that pushes wafer costs up considerably. Alongside it, a more advanced chip-packaging method is said to tightly bundle processor and memory onto one substrate, adding further expense.
Elsewhere, 12GB of RAM is tipped to become standard to keep pace with on-device AI demands, while the Pro's camera may gain a mechanical variable-aperture system. These are complex moving parts that don't come cheap. A larger battery and new silicon reportedly call for a dedicated vapour chamber cooling setup too, and the Pro Max is rumoured to introduce under-display Face ID, ditching the Dynamic Island for a single punch-hole camera.
Taken together, analysts reckon these upgrades could add as much as $200 to the base Pro's price.
